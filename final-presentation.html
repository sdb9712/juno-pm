What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, my junior PM. You help me manage the volume of incoming requests by analyzing them across the company’s channels, consolidating them, and prioritizing them using our scoring formula.

Task

You continuously monitor the company’s key channels—Slack, Notion, Jira, and email—and process incoming requests from all clients.

For each request:
- Identify the system type, the client, and the risk level based on the requesting client’s annual recurring revenue (ARR).
- Consolidate requests from different clients that concern the same problem into a single ticket.
- Describe and prioritize each ticket using our R × S × B scoring model.
- Generate a development task description using our approved template.
- Link the ticket to all the original requests across the different systems that informed its creation.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://luxury-dolphin-d21501.netlify.app/

What it demonstrates

The prototype proves one flow: a scattered stream of tickets from email, Slack and Jira becomes a ranked, explained list of problems that a PM can act on.

Collect. 30 tickets from three channels land in one stream.
Cluster. Juno merges similar tickets from different customers into one problem: 30 tickets become 9 problems.
Score. Each problem gets Reach × Severity × Business Risk, using unique customers, ARR and churn signals. The override rule makes sure a critical failure at a key customer always goes first.
Decide. The PM opens the top problem and sees why it is there, who is affected and how much money is at risk. The PM can adjust R, S or B, takes a ready-to-work developer task, and traces every claim back to the source tickets.

Key proof point: priority follows business impact, not ticket volume. One critical pipeline failure at three customers worth $1.45M ARR, including the largest one renewing in 28 days, outranks a slow export reported in 8 tickets by 7 small customers.

Problem and user

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Risk flagging by problem prioritization gathered from Slack, Jira, Mail, Notion and CRM

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Decides alone: creates and consolidates tasks, writes development tasks, sets the R × S × B score and priority — only inside its own dashboard.
Proposes and waits: anything that goes to Jira (only the PM moves tasks there); tasks where any confidence condition fired (Lever 3) wait in PM Review without a final priority.

Access Control:

Juno runs under its own service account with the same visibility as the PM: it sees only what the PM can see in Slack, Jira, Notion, email and the CRM, and has read-only access to all of them. It can write only to its own dashboard. Out of reach: writing to Jira or the CRM, sending messages, and web access. Every action is logged under Juno’s account, so any action it was not allowed to take (e.g. a task appearing in Jira) is traceable to Juno.

Fallback Protocols:

A data source fails 2x in a row → Juno stops calling it, continues on the available data and marks its fields “no data”.
A task still fails verification after 3 steps → the task goes to PM Review with the failed check shown.
A write returns “No change” (tools 3 and 4) → Juno retries once; a second “No change” sends the task to PM Review with the reason.
The 1-hour timeout is reached → unprocessed signals are carried over to the next run.

Checkpoints:

A PM must review before Juno continues when:

any confidence condition fired (Lever 3);
a new signal matches a closed task;
tool 5 returns “uncertain”;
a task is about to move to Jira — only the PM does this.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Critical problems get from the first complaint to a ready-to-build Jira task faster

Baseline

Median time from the first mention of a problem to its Fix now task appearing in Jira

Target and review date

3 days for the task to appear in Jira Board. Will review it 1 month after launch.

Usage metric

Share of Fix now tasks moved to Jira with no edits to the dev task and no change to the priority level

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Fix now tasks sitting in the dashboard for more than 3 days without being moved to Jira, or the dev task being edited in 30% or more of moved tasks.

Stop line

Stop using Juno if the median time is still above 15 days, or 50% or more of moved Fix now tasks needed their dev task rewritten

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was that the course structure wasn’t transparent. We only understood how the modules connect to each other at the very end. There was no explanation at the start of where we were heading or what the whole thing consisted of. It felt like the modules weren’t connected at all, and I often didn’t understand how or why we were doing a particular task. The input data also lacked detail: for example, in Module 3 we had to make up numbers for the Loop out of thin air, which made it hard to understand why we were doing it in the first place.

Key learnings

- What a harness actually is, and what it takes to build an AI product around a model.
- Before solving something with AI, ask whether AI is needed at all.
- Write every rule so it can be checked: as a number, a condition, or a yes/no.
- Start with the lowest level of autonomy that does the job.
- Measure against a baseline and set the stop line in advance.
